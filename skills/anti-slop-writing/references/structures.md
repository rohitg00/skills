# Structure Cleanup Reference

Use this file when a draft has the right words but still feels staged, formulaic, or generated. Structural slop usually comes from predictable rhetorical moves: setup, reversal, dramatic fragment, permission, lesson.

## Binary Contrast

Pattern:

```text
Not X. Y.
It is not X. It is Y.
X is not the problem. Y is.
The answer is not X, it is Y.
```

Why it fails: the structure telegraphs a reveal before delivering the point.

Rewrite:

```text
Y is the problem.
Y matters because <specific reason>.
```

Keep contrast only when both sides are necessary and specific.

## Negative Listing

Pattern:

```text
It was not X.
It was not Y.
It was Z.
```

Why it fails: it creates a runway before the actual claim.

Rewrite:

```text
It was Z. X and Y only looked like causes because <reason>.
```

## Rhetorical Scaffolding

Pattern:

```text
What if the real issue is X?
Here's what I mean.
Think about it.
```

Why it fails: the prose announces insight instead of delivering it.

Rewrite:

```text
The issue is X.
<specific evidence or example>
```

## Dramatic Fragmentation

Pattern:

```text
Speed.
Trust.
Distribution.
That's the difference.
```

Why it fails: fragments create artificial gravity.

Rewrite:

```text
Speed, trust, and distribution decide the outcome.
```

If a short sentence is needed, use one. Do not stack them.

## Formula Hook

Pattern:

```text
Nobody talks about X.
The uncomfortable truth about X.
The secret to X.
The one thing people miss about X.
```

Why it fails: the hook borrows tension from a familiar template.

Rewrite:

```text
<Specific claim about X>.
```

Better hooks use an event, result, constraint, mistake, or concrete observation.

## Permission Ending

Pattern:

```text
And that's okay.
And that's the point.
And that's enough.
```

Why it fails: it tells readers how to feel.

Rewrite:

End on the last useful sentence. If the post needs a conclusion, make it a specific consequence or decision rule.

## Fake Lesson Arc

Pattern:

```text
I used to think X.
Then I learned Y.
Now I know Z.
```

Why it fails: the structure can flatten real experience into a tidy fable.

Rewrite:

Use the actual scene:

```text
I changed my mind after <event>. <Specific detail> made X hard to defend.
```

## False Agency

Pattern:

```text
The market rewards clarity.
The data tells a story.
The decision emerged.
The culture shifted.
The product found its audience.
```

Why it fails: inanimate nouns do human work.

Rewrite:

Name the actor:

```text
Buyers paid for the simpler option.
The team changed the rollout plan.
Managers changed the incentive.
Users shared the product with peers.
```

If the actor is unknown, use "people", "teams", "buyers", "readers", or "users".

## Passive Voice

Pattern:

```text
Mistakes were made.
The feature was shipped.
The decision was reached.
It is believed that...
```

Why it fails: passive voice hides responsibility and drains motion.

Rewrite:

```text
The team made the mistake.
We shipped the feature.
The founder chose the slower path.
Analysts believe...
```

Keep passive voice only when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.

## Abstract Noun Stack

Pattern:

```text
The transformation of operational excellence requires alignment across strategic priorities.
```

Why it fails: too many abstractions compete.

Rewrite:

```text
Teams improve operations when leaders choose one priority and fund it.
```

Rule: use one abstraction per sentence unless the audience requires technical precision.

## Three-Beat Rhythm

Pattern:

```text
Build. Ship. Learn.
Fast, simple, reliable.
People, process, technology.
```

Why it fails: repeated trios sound templated.

Rewrite:

Use one precise item, two contrasting items, or a full sentence.

```text
Ship the smallest version that proves the behavior.
```

## Question Answered Immediately

Pattern:

```text
Why does this matter?
Because it changes everything.
```

Why it fails: the question adds ceremony.

Rewrite:

```text
This matters because it removes the manual review step.
```

## Over-Neat Paragraph Shape

Pattern:

```text
One short line.

One short line.

One short line.

Punchline.
```

Why it fails: identical paragraph rhythm makes writing feel manufactured.

Rewrite:

Mix sentence length and paragraph length. Let explanation breathe where the idea needs detail.

## Checklist For Structural Cleanup

- [ ] Does the draft open with setup instead of content?
- [ ] Does it use a fake reversal?
- [ ] Does it list what something is not before saying what it is?
- [ ] Does it rely on rhetorical questions?
- [ ] Does it stack fragments for drama?
- [ ] Does it hide the actor?
- [ ] Does every paragraph have the same rhythm?
- [ ] Does it end with a slogan instead of a useful thought?
- [ ] Could the strongest sentence be moved to the top?
- [ ] Could the post lose 20 percent without losing meaning?
